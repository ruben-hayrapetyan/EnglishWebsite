Why the kits look like this

Each tool is there because of what learning research says works, not because it looks good in a study app.

  • Quizzes, not rereading. Of ten common study techniques, practice testing and spacing study out over time were the only two rated high utility; rereading and highlighting were rated low (Dunlosky et al., 2013).
  • Flashcards that space themselves. Terms you mark "Still learning" come back sooner; terms you know drop out until the next round.
  • Feedback that explains. Every quiz option says why it's right or wrong. Explanations beat simply being told the answer, most of all on new questions (Van der Kleij et al., 2015; Butler et al., 2013).
  • Saying how sure you are. Mistakes made with high confidence are the ones people correct best once they see the answer (Butterfield & Metcalfe, 2001), so the quiz flags them.
  • Explaining it back. Putting an idea in your own words before checking (self-explanation) is a moderate-utility technique in the same review.
